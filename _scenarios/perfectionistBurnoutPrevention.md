## $main Lukas is caught by automated tracking before reaching pre-exam panic

Lukas Weber is in his final Bachelor semester, working on his thesis and remaining compulsory modules while fueling himself with energy drinks through late nights of cramming. He exhibits severe perfectionism and procrastination, ignoring the early physical red flags of mental fatigue. As he approaches pre-exam panic, he bypasses traditional mental health apps because he finds long journals tedious. The platform's gamified, automated tracking features catch his downward burnout spiral early. The system triggers a clear, low-stimulation UI mode that keeps him grounded and delivers focused relaxation choices.

## $alternative Lukas overrides the low-stimulation mode during a rush phase

Lukas encounters the low-stimulation UI mode triggered by his detected burnout spiral, but he feels he has a critical handle on his thesis formatting and wishes to temporarily bypass the restriction to finish a section. He selects an override option that requires a quick confirmation step. The system grants a temporary 30-minute window of full app access while scheduling a mandatory check-in prompt immediately afterward to ensure he does not completely neglect his recovery.

## $exception Lukas enters false positive data into his gamified tracking metrics

Lukas engages with the gamified tracking feature while feeling rushed and absentmindedly clicks random options that indicate high well-being, masking his actual state of severe pre-exam panic. Because the system relies entirely on his conflicting quick-inputs, it fails to recognize his true burnout risk and does not trigger the necessary low-stimulation safety mode. Lukas continues his unhealthy cramming routine unchecked until a physical exhaustion symptom forces him to stop, highlighting a limitation when manual tracking inputs are inaccurate.
