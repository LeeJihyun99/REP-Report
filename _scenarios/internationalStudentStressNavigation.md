## $main Mateo uses the mobile-first app for discreet stress tracking and coping guidance

Mateo Silva is in his student apartment in Germany, six months after moving from Brazil. He is facing complex administrative structures, studying in a foreign language, and experiencing homesickness. He feels severe anxiety and isolation mounting as upcoming deadlines approach while his friends are in a different time zone. Seeking discretion due to his fear of institutional stigma, he opens the mobile-first mental health application to track his well-being anonymously. The system provides proactive, simple guidance through a low-barrier interface that avoids complex UI navigation. Mateo receives tailored stress-management recommendations and accesses local academic coping resources directly through the app.

## $alternative Mateo experiences a language barrier with localized support text

Mateo opens the application during a period of high anxiety, but encounters a localized support resource description written in advanced German terminology that he struggles to comprehend in his foreign-language study environment. He uses the app's language setting adjustment option to switch the interface text to simplified English explanations. The system instantly updates the resource terminology, allowing Mateo to understand the coping instructions clearly without adding to his cognitive burden.

## $exception Mateo experiences connectivity failure during an acute stress peak

Mateo experiences a sudden surge of homesickness and anxiety late at night, but his apartment's internet connection drops unexpectedly just as he attempts to open the mobile application. Because the app's live cloud features are temporarily unreachable, Mateo initially feels stranded. Fortunately, the application features an offline mode containing pre-downloaded micro-exercises and emergency breathing techniques, allowing him to access basic grounding tools locally on his device until network connectivity is restored.
