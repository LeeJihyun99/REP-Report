### Contact with Domain Experts

To complement the user-centered elicitation activities, we attempted to conduct an interview with a domain expert from the psychological counseling services of the Kölner Studierendenwerk by sending an email to them on the 22 July 2026.

On 4 August 2026, Head of Counseling, Children and Social Services, Kölner Studierendenwerk replied that, due to the large number of similar requests from students and limited counseling resources, the organization has a policy of consistently declining requests for interviews, surveys, or other research participation related to student projects. Consequently, it was not possible to conduct the planned expert interview.

To compensate for the missing interview, the project team conducted a structured literature review of recent (2024–2026) peer-reviewed publications and expert recommendations on student mental health, university counseling services, and AI-assisted mental health support. These findings were used as the primary source of domain expert knowledge during the requirements elicitation process.


## Results

## Findings from Literature Review

### Q1. What are the most common mental health challenges that students seek help for?

Recent literature consistently identifies anxiety, depression, academic stress, burnout, loneliness, adjustment difficulties, and career uncertainty as the most common reasons why university students seek psychological support. The reviewed studies also highlight an increasing need for preventive mental health support rather than only crisis intervention.

**References:** madrid2025, nyakhar2025


### Q2. Are there particular periods during the semester when students experience more stress?

Experts report that examination periods, assignment deadlines, project submissions, and academic transitions consistently increase stress levels and counseling demand. Continuous monitoring and early intervention are therefore recommended.

**References:** madrid2025, nyakhar2025


### Q3. What prevents students from seeking professional help?

The main barriers include stigma, concerns about confidentiality, long waiting times, limited counselor availability, scheduling conflicts, and uncertainty about whether their problems are serious enough to seek help.

**References:** madrid2025, wu2025


### Q5. What could be improved?

Experts recommend improving accessibility, reducing waiting times, providing support outside counseling hours, integrating AI into existing counseling services, and offering personalized digital support between counseling sessions.

**References:** wu2025, jmir2024


### Q6. Do students often need support outside counseling hours?

Yes. Students frequently seek support during evenings, weekends, and examination periods when counseling services are unavailable. AI can provide immediate first-line support during these periods while referring high-risk cases to professionals.

**References:** madrid2025, jmir2024

### Q7. What is your opinion on AI as an additional mental health support tool?

The reviewed literature consistently concludes that AI should complement—not replace—professional mental health services. AI is appropriate for psychoeducation, emotional support, stress management, journaling, and self-reflection.

**References:** nyakhar2025, llmreview2024


### Q8. In which situations could AI be helpful?

Experts recommend AI for daily mood check-ins, stress monitoring, CBT-based coping exercises, journaling, habit formation, academic stress management, and directing students to appropriate university counseling services.

**References:** nyakhar2025, wu2025

### Q9. When should AI refer users to professionals?

AI should immediately recommend professional support when detecting suicidal ideation, self-harm, severe depression, psychosis, panic attacks, or other high-risk situations.

**References:** jmir2024, llmreview2024


### Q10. What concerns exist regarding AI mental health applications?

The most common concerns include inaccurate recommendations, hallucinations, privacy risks, lack of empathy, algorithmic bias, overreliance on AI, and insufficient transparency.

**References:** jmir2024, llmreview2024

### Q11. Which features are considered most valuable?

Frequently recommended features include personalized AI conversations, mood tracking, stress monitoring, personalized coping strategies, mindfulness exercises, habit building, and seamless integration with university counseling services.

**References:** nyakhar2025, wu2025


### Q12. What resources should always be available?

Experts recommend including emergency contacts, crisis hotlines, university counseling information, evidence-based self-help resources, psychoeducation materials, and referral pathways to professional care.

**References:** madrid2025, jmir2024


### Q13. How should healthy habits be encouraged?

Experts recommend adaptive reminders, personalized goals, positive reinforcement, small achievable habits, and evidence-based behavior change techniques instead of excessive notifications or pressure.

**References:** madrid2025, wu2025


### Q14–Q16. Privacy & Trust

Privacy, user consent, transparent AI recommendations, anonymous usage, secure data storage, and user control over stored conversations are consistently identified as essential requirements.

**References:** jmir2024, llmreview2024



### Q17. What is the most important feature of an ideal student mental health application?

The reviewed literature consistently highlights personalization, evidence-based recommendations, continuity across conversations, and seamless integration with human counseling services as the most valuable characteristics.

**References:** nyakhar2025, wu2025
