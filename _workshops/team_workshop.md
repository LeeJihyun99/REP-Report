## Details

The ideas generated during the workshop are grouped into categories. The participant first generated ideas through brainstorming, followed by a brainstorming paradox to identify undesirable situations and potential risks. Finally, the proposed features were prioritized according to their perceived importance.

## Results: List of Ideas & Questions


## Question 1: What features should an AI-assisted mental health application for students provide?

### Mental Health & Emotional Support

- Help users recognize unhealthy coping behaviors (e.g., instant dopamine activities) and encourage healthier long-term alternatives.
- Guide users out of emotionally overwhelming situations by promoting rational decision-making.
- Encourage users to engage in sustainable activities that improve long-term well-being.
- Encourage and motivate users to exercise by explaining scientifically proven physical and mental health benefits.
- Provide scientifically validated exercises and mental health programs.
- Recommend meditation or prayer sessions based on detected stress levels.



### AI Personalization

- Start with an initial consultation session with a professional therapist or counselor before AI personalization begins.
- Personalize AI recommendations based on information gathered during the initial consultation.
- Remember previous conversations and continuously adapt recommendations.
- Provide highly personalized recommendations instead of generic responses.
- Behave like a psychologist rather than a social media influencer, creating trustworthy and meaningful conversations.


### Scientific & Evidence-based Guidance

- Diagnose users' problems using scientifically validated psychological and medical knowledge.
- Explain recommendations transparently instead of providing generic motivational quotes.
- Support every recommendation with research findings or professional knowledge.
- Provide rational, logical advice rather than purely emotional encouragement.
- Clearly explain why a recommendation is generated.


### Student Support

- Help users avoid comparing themselves to others.
- Provide personalized academic recommendations when students feel overwhelmed by assignments.
- Encourage users to build sustainable study habits.
- Support students during long-term academic stress rather than only during crises.



### Safety & Crisis Support

- Detect panic attacks or severe emotional crises.
- Recommend contacting professional support when necessary.
- Automatically suggest or initiate emergency support if the user's condition becomes critical and they are alone.

## Highest Priority Features

### 1. Meditation Exercises

The participant considered guided meditation and mindfulness exercises the most important feature, as they provide immediate support during stressful situations and help users regulate their emotions.

### 2. Study Stress Management

Support specifically tailored to academic stress was ranked as the second most important feature. The participant emphasized that university-related stress differs from general stress and requires specialized guidance.

### 3. Personalized Coping Strategies

The participant valued recommendations that are adapted to individual situations instead of providing generic advice.

### 4. Goal & Habit Tracking

Tracking goals and developing healthy study habits were considered important for improving long-term mental well-being.

### 5. Stress Monitoring

Monitoring stress levels over time was regarded as an effective way to identify unhealthy patterns before they become severe.
